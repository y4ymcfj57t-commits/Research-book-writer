## Verification Notes

### Highest Confidence Claims (Primary Documents)

1. **1911 Letter to Bess Wallace** (C-02-001, C-02-002, C-02-003)
   - Original document at National Archives
   - Published in Robert Ferrell, *Dear Bess* (1983)
   - Catalog ID: 157638923

2. **1937 and 1939 Letters** (C-04-003, C-04-004)
   - Original documents at Truman Library
   - Cited in multiple secondary sources

3. **Executive Orders** (C-06-001, C-06-006)
   - Official government records
   - Published in Federal Register
   - Available at National Archives

4. **NAACP Speech** (C-06-003, C-06-004)
   - Multiple audio/video recordings exist
   - Transcript at Truman Library and American Rhetoric

5. **Congressional Actions** (C-04-001, C-06-005)
   - Official Congressional Record

### Moderate Confidence Claims (Scholarly Sources)

Claims rated PROBABLE rely on:
- Scholarly secondary sources (NPS, King Institute)
- Journalist accounts (Seattle Times, Washington Post)
- Biographies with primary research (McCullough, Gergel)
- Institutional sources (Truman Library materials)

These sources are generally reliable but lack the certainty of primary documents.

### Contested Claims

**C-03-001: KKK Membership Payment**
- Sources agree Truman paid $10 to a Klan organizer
- Disputed: whether he signed membership card, took oath
- Consensus: He was never formally initiated
- Resolution: Present as "brief involvement" with uncertainty acknowledged

### Claims Requiring Additional Verification

1. **C-05-004: "My God!" Reaction**
   - Single source: Walter White memoir
   - White had interest in portraying meeting dramatically
   - Subsequent actions consistent with claimed reaction
   - Flagged as PROBABLE pending additional corroboration

2. **C-08-001 through C-08-004: Post-Presidency Statements**
   - Rely primarily on King Institute encyclopedia entry
   - Would benefit from primary documentation of 1960 statements
   - Pattern consistent with documented attitudes

## Red Team Assessment

### Most Vulnerable Claims

1. **Truman's "genuine" moral outrage at Woodard**
   - Attack: Could have been strategic performance
   - Defense: Consistent pattern of subsequent action; risks taken
   - Status: Interpretation flagged as uncertain

2. **Transformation vs. Calculation**
   - Attack: All actions had political benefit
   - Defense: Risks were real; 80% opposed agenda
   - Status: Multiple motivations acknowledged

### Strongest Claims

1. **Documentary racism in letters**
   - Primary documents in Truman's hand
   - Published by reputable editor (Ferrell)
   - Consistent pattern across decades

2. **Executive Order 9981**
   - Official government document
   - Consequences documented
   - Implementation verified

---

## Conclusion

This biographical study rests on a solid evidentiary foundation. The core claims—Truman's documented racism, his family's slaveholding heritage, and his unprecedented presidential civil rights actions—are well-supported by primary sources and corroborated by multiple scholars.

Areas of uncertainty are acknowledged and flagged. Interpretive claims about motivation and meaning are presented as interpretations, not facts.

The paradox at the heart of the study—personal prejudice coexisting with public achievement—is documented rather than resolved. The evidence supports the complexity rather than any simple narrative.

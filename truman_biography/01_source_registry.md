# Source Registry

## Harry S. Truman: Slavery, Racism, and Civil Rights

This registry documents all sources consulted for this biographical study, organized by type and assessed for reliability.

---

## Primary Sources

### S-DOC-1911-001: Letter from Harry S. Truman to Bess Wallace
- **Date:** June 22, 1911
- **Type:** Personal correspondence
- **Repository:** Harry S. Truman Library; National Archives (Catalog ID: 157638923)
- **Published in:** Robert Ferrell, ed., *Dear Bess: The Letters from Harry to Bess Truman, 1910-1959* (1983), p. 39
- **Content:** Contains statement "I think one man is just as good as another so long as he's honest and decent and not a nigger or a Chinaman" and extended white supremacist views
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Original document in Truman's hand
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - Earliest documented expression of racial views

### S-DOC-1937-001: Letter from Harry S. Truman to Margaret Truman
- **Date:** 1937
- **Type:** Personal correspondence
- **Repository:** Truman Library
- **Content:** Describes White House waiters as "an army of coons"
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Original document
- **Significance:** HIGH - Shows persistence of racist language as Senator

### S-DOC-1939-001: Letter from Harry S. Truman to Bess Truman
- **Date:** 1939
- **Type:** Personal correspondence
- **Repository:** Truman Library
- **Content:** Reference to "nigger picnic day" (possibly Juneteenth celebration)
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Original document
- **Significance:** HIGH - Shows continued use of slurs during Senate years

### S-GOV-1946-001: Executive Order 9808
- **Date:** December 5, 1946
- **Type:** Government record
- **Repository:** National Archives; Federal Register
- **Content:** Establishes President's Committee on Civil Rights
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Official government document
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - First presidential civil rights commission

### S-GOV-1947-001: "To Secure These Rights" Report
- **Date:** October 29, 1947
- **Type:** Government report
- **Repository:** Truman Library; widely published
- **URL:** https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/to-secure-these-rights
- **Content:** 178-page report with 34 recommendations for civil rights reform
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Official government document
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - Blueprint for civil rights legislation

### S-GOV-1948-001: Special Message to Congress on Civil Rights
- **Date:** February 2, 1948
- **Type:** Presidential message
- **Repository:** Truman Library Public Papers
- **URL:** https://www.trumanlibrary.gov/library/public-papers/20/special-message-congress-civil-rights
- **Content:** First presidential civil rights message to Congress; ten-point program
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Official government document
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - First presidential civil rights legislative program

### S-GOV-1948-002: Executive Order 9980
- **Date:** July 26, 1948
- **Type:** Government record
- **Repository:** National Archives
- **Content:** Desegregates federal workforce
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Official government document
- **Significance:** HIGH - Major executive action

### S-GOV-1948-003: Executive Order 9981
- **Date:** July 26, 1948
- **Type:** Government record
- **Repository:** National Archives
- **URL:** https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/executive-order-9981
- **Content:** Abolishes discrimination in armed forces
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Official government document
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - Military desegregation order

### S-SPE-1947-001: Address Before the NAACP
- **Date:** June 29, 1947
- **Type:** Speech transcript/audio
- **Repository:** Truman Library; American Rhetoric
- **URL:** https://www.americanrhetoric.com/speeches/harrystrumannaacp.htm
- **Content:** First presidential address to NAACP; "all Americans" declaration
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Multiple recordings and transcripts exist
- **Significance:** CRITICAL - Historic presidential address

## Secondary Sources - Scholarly

### S-PUB-1991-001: "The Conversion of Harry Truman"
- **Author:** William E. Leuchtenburg
- **Publication:** *American Heritage*, November 1991, Vol. 42, Issue 7
- **URL:** https://www.americanheritage.com/conversion-harry-truman
- **Content:** Influential article on Truman's evolution on civil rights
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Respected historian, primary source citations
- **Significance:** HIGH - Established "conversion" narrative framework

### S-PUB-1992-001: *Truman*
- **Author:** David McCullough
- **Publication:** Simon & Schuster, 1992
- **Content:** Pulitzer Prize-winning biography; comprehensive treatment
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Extensive primary research
- **Significance:** HIGH - Definitive general biography

### S-PUB-1974-001: "Kansas City Blacks, Harry Truman and the Pendergast Machine"
- **Author:** Larry Grothaus
- **Publication:** *Missouri Historical Review* 69, no. 1 (October 1974): 65-82
- **Content:** Scholarly analysis of Black political involvement in Pendergast organization
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Peer-reviewed scholarly article
- **Significance:** HIGH - Key source for Pendergast-era politics

### S-PUB-2002-001: *Harry Truman and Civil Rights: Moral Courage and Political Risks*
- **Author:** Michael Gardner
- **Publication:** Southern Illinois University Press, 2002
- **Content:** Focused study of Truman's civil rights record
- **Reliability:** MEDIUM-HIGH - Criticized for minimizing early racism
- **Significance:** HIGH - Major focused study of topic

### S-PUB-2019-001: *Unexampled Courage*
- **Author:** Richard Gergel
- **Publication:** Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 2019
- **Content:** Isaac Woodard case and its impact on Truman
- **Reliability:** HIGH - Federal judge with access to court records
- **Significance:** HIGH - Definitive account of Woodard incident

## Source Gaps Identified

1. **Complete survey of Truman correspondence** - Only selected letters widely quoted; full collection not systematically reviewed for this study
2. **Pendergast-era internal communications** - Limited documentary evidence of racial calculations
3. **Private diary entries 1946-1948** - Limited access to unedited versions
4. **Interview transcripts with Truman about civil rights motivations** - Reliant on secondary accounts

## Citation Format Standards

This project uses Chicago Manual of Style, 17th Edition:

**Archival Sources:**
> [Document description], [Date], [Collection Name], [Box/Folder if applicable], [Repository], [City].

**Published Primary Sources:**
> Author Last, First. *Title*. Place: Publisher, Year. Page.

**Government Documents:**
> Executive Order [Number], [Date], Federal Register [Vol.], [Page].

**Web Sources:**
> Author/Organization. "Title." Website Name. URL (accessed [Date]).
